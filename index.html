CO₂ Ammonia Stripper — Design Decisions

Part of a year-long UCL capstone designing an integrated offshore-onshore green ammonia plant covering process route selection, heat integration, LCA, TEA, and HAZOP. My individual design unit was a high-pressure falling-film CO₂ stripper for the urea synthesis loop, designed to decompose ammonium carbamate (a water-soluble intermediate) and recover ammonia, at 152 bar and 182°C. Here are five decisions from my design process, each grounded in hard-earned simulation data from hundreds of parametric runs carried out in ASPEN Plus.

Decision 01 - Thermodynamic model SR-Polar selected over ENRTL-RK

Both models were validated against three experimental datasets: water-urea density at high pressure, NH₃-water and CO₂-water phase equilibria, and equilibrium urea and carbamate conversion. SR-Polar won on all three. Critically, ENRTL-RK overestimated CO₂ solubility in water by a factor of 10, which would have disrupted the accuracy of the simulation's stripping driving force. At 19 mol% urea, SR-Polar's density error was 0.66% versus 3.42% for ENRTL-RK, an important metric to track due to it's impact on the accuracy of sizing calculations. This choice underpinned everything downstream: McCabe-Thiele hand sizing, the RADFRAC simulation, and the parametric sensitivity analysis.

Decision 02 - Column stage count 8 stages chosen over 9 or 12

A parametric study across 4, 7, 8, 9, and 12 equilibrium stages at fixed heat duty (set at the estimated amount required for the desired decomposition) showed sharply diminishing returns past 8. The jump from 4 to 8 stages gave a significant efficiency gain; adding 4 more stages to reach 12 gave a fraction of that improvement. 8 stages satisfies the design requirement while keeping the column height and Safurex® tube cost within reason.

Decision 03 - Operating point 60 kmol/hr CO₂, 0.16 MW/stage

The full sensitivity analysis over CO₂ flow rate and heat duty showed both variables increase efficiency roughly linearly with no strong interaction. The chosen operating point sits at the knee of diminishing returns on the CO₂ axis, while keeping the liquid film temperature 8°C below the biuret formation threshold at 190°C.

Decision 04 - Thermal limitation Pushing harder makes decomposition worse

Varying NH₃ stripping efficiency revealed a counter-intuitive result: higher stripping also reduces carbamate decomposition. More aggressive vaporisation removes heat from the liquid film, cooling it and suppressing the endothermic decomposition reaction. This coupling is why the original ambitious 80% target was abandoned - the heat input required would close the margin to biuret formation at 190°C, only 8°C above design temperature. The final target of 72.2% was set at the point where stripping performance and thermal stability are balanced, while remaining well within acceptable limits from literature.

Decision 05 - Column sizing & steam utility MP steam and 10m tubes at 7.5m heated length

Three steam utilities were evaluated. LP steam is physically impossible at these conditions - the saturation temperature is below the process temperature giving a negative driving force. HP steam delivers more than enough heat but the 91°C temperature difference risks local overheating of the liquid film, accelerating corrosion of the Safurex® tubes and promoting biuret formation. MP steam at 19 barg gives a controlled 29°C ΔT, preventing hot spots while delivering the full 0.96 MW across 13 m² of transfer area. The overall heat transfer coefficient of 2.5 kW/m²K was taken from Stamicarbon plant data for Safurex® tubing, which accounts for the urea solution film resistance.
